# One Word From Your Ex

Entry for the make.fun bounty "One Word From Your Ex".

## What it is

The program writes exactly one word to `/output/entry.txt` — the word
an ex would use to describe their former partner.

**The word: `Exhausting`**

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It creates `/output` if needed and writes the word plus a trailing
newline to `/output/entry.txt`. No network, no dependencies, fully
deterministic — every run produces the same byte-identical output.

## Why "Exhausting" wins

Against the judge's criteria, in order:

1. **Exactly one real word an ex would say.** "Exhausting" is the word
   people actually reach for when a relationship wore them out — not an
   insult, a diagnosis. It is instantly believable as an ex's verdict.
2. **Funny because it is true.** The laugh comes from recognition, not
   from a punchline. Everyone has either been the exhausting one or
   dated one, and the word lands as a wince-laugh.
3. **Painfully honest.** It stings precisely because it cannot be
   argued with: it describes the *experience* of being with the person,
   not a single flaw. "Dramatic" or "Intense" can be worn as a badge;
   nobody brags about being exhausting.
4. **Lands instantly.** One word, no setup, no explanation needed — the
   whole relationship is in it.
5. **Relatable.** It is universal across ages, genders, and kinds of
   breakup, which is why it beats narrower picks like "Avoidant"
   (therapy jargon, less funny) or "Unavailable" (flat, no sting).
